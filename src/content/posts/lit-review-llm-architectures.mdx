# Literature Review: LLM Architectures

## Overview

The transformer remains the backbone of modern LLMs, but frontier architecture work has shifted from inventing a new block to making the block economically viable at extreme scale. The pressure points are clear: active parameters, memory bandwidth, KV-cache growth, routing communication, long-context attention, training stability, and numeric precision.

Self-attention starts from

$$
\mathrm{Attn}(Q,K,V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V,
$$

which is expressive but quadratic in sequence length. During decoding, the KV cache grows as

$$
O(n \cdot L \cdot h_{kv} \cdot d_k),
$$

for context length $n$, layers $L$, KV heads $h_{kv}$, and head dimension $d_k$. At million-token context, KV memory and per-token attention cost become first-order architecture constraints. DeepSeek-V4 is useful as a synthesis point because it combines the major frontier moves in one system: MoE scaling, compressed long-context attention, residual-stream conditioning, optimizer co-design, and quantization-aware numerics.

## Conceptual Map

- **Sparse parameter scaling:** MoE models increase total capacity while keeping active parameters per token bounded. The tradeoff moves from dense matmul cost to routing quality and all-to-all communication.
- **KV-cache compression:** MQA/GQA share K/V heads; MLA compresses K/V into latent form; heavily compressed attention schemes trade exact token-level memory for approximate long-range access.
- **Sparse long-context attention:** sliding windows, block sparsity, retrieval-like attention, and compressed sparse attention reduce the quadratic term by making not every token attend to every other token.
- **Residual and depth stabilization:** residual streams are no longer treated as passive skip connections; systems like Hyper-Connections learn richer mixing while trying to preserve geometry.
- **Optimizer and numeric co-design:** optimizers such as Muon and low-precision training such as FP8/FP4 QAT are now architectural choices because they determine which blocks are stable and affordable.

The field is converging on a budgeted objective: maximize quality per active FLOP, KV byte, and training token, not quality per total parameter.

## Mathematical Setup

For MoE feed-forward layers, a router maps token representation $x$ to expert weights:

$$
p(e \mid x) = \mathrm{softmax}(W_r x), \qquad
\mathrm{MoE}(x) = \sum_{e \in \mathrm{TopK}(p)} p(e \mid x) f_e(x).
$$

Only $k$ experts are active per token. Total parameters can grow with the number of experts $E$, while active compute scales with $k$.

For KV compression, the goal is to replace full per-head cached keys and values with a smaller state $c_t$:

$$
c_t = P_\downarrow [k_t; v_t], \qquad
[\widehat{k}_t; \widehat{v}_t] = P_\uparrow c_t,
$$

or to avoid reconstructing full K/V except where needed. The approximation must preserve enough attention geometry that downstream reasoning and retrieval across long contexts do not collapse.

For manifold-constrained residual mixing, the broad idea is to replace a simple update

$$
x_{\ell+1} = x_\ell + F_\ell(x_\ell)
$$

with a learned mixing operator constrained to keep representations well-conditioned:

$$
x_{\ell+1} = M_\ell(x_0,\ldots,x_\ell,F_\ell(x_\ell)), \qquad M_\ell \in \mathcal{M}.
$$

The exact implementation is paper-specific, but the design intent is general: improve depth and context stability by controlling residual-stream geometry.

## Algorithms and Design Patterns

**Long-context architecture stack.**

1. Reduce KV cache with shared, latent, or heavily compressed K/V representations.
2. Use sparse or compressed attention for the long tail of context.
3. Keep high-fidelity attention where local or recent tokens matter.
4. Train with context-parallel kernels and post-train on long-context tasks.
5. Store or page KV state when the context no longer fits in device memory.

**MoE scaling stack.**

1. Route each token to a small expert set.
2. Add load-balancing losses or capacity constraints.
3. Optimize all-to-all communication.
4. Measure active parameters and inference FLOPs, not only total parameters.

**Architecture-numerics co-design.**

DeepSeek-V4's FP4 QAT integration is not a post-hoc compression pass. It is evidence that architecture, optimizer, and numerical format are merging: if a model is intended to run at low precision, training must expose the model to those constraints early enough that the learned computation adapts.

## Empirical Picture

DeepSeek-V4 reports a 1.6T-total / 49B-active Pro model and a 284B-total / 13B-active Flash model with native one-million-token context. The central empirical claim is efficiency at long context: at 1M tokens, the architecture uses a fraction of DeepSeek-V3.2's single-token inference FLOPs and KV cache while improving knowledge, reasoning, and agentic benchmarks.

The result is best read as a systems-architecture bundle rather than a single ablation. Hybrid compressed attention reduces long-context cost; MoE controls active compute; manifold-constrained Hyper-Connections target training stability; Muon affects convergence; FP4 QAT changes the numeric deployment surface. The evidence says frontier gains increasingly come from co-design across these layers.

## Open Problems

- **Faithful million-token evaluation:** long-context benchmarks must test retrieval, synthesis, and robustness, not just needle finding.
- **Compression failure modes:** KV compression can silently erase rare but decisive evidence.
- **Router stability:** MoE load balance, expert specialization, and communication overhead remain coupled.
- **Optimizer portability:** Muon-style gains may depend on architecture, scale, and implementation details.
- **Quantization-native architecture:** FP4 training changes which activations, residual paths, and normalization schemes are robust.
