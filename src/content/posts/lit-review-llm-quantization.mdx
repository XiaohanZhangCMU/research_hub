# Literature Review: LLM Quantization

## Overview

LLM quantization asks how far we can reduce numeric precision before the model's computation stops approximating the full-precision network. The deployment motivation is straightforward: memory bandwidth and KV-cache size dominate serving cost. A 70B FP16 model needs roughly 140 GB for weights alone; INT4 weights bring that under 40 GB before activation and cache overhead. The scientific problem is harder: LLMs have activation outliers, fragile downstream behavior, long-context cache pressure, and local calibration objectives that often fail to predict end-task quality.

The field has moved through three regimes. Post-training quantization (PTQ) calibrates a trained model cheaply, but aggressive W4A4 settings expose cumulative error and objective mismatch. Quantization-aware training (QAT) restores end-to-end alignment by training through simulated quantization, but conventional QAT is expensive at LLM scale. The current frontier tries to keep QAT's quality while removing its cost: data-free distillation, block-wise training plus end-to-end scale tuning, and forward-only zeroth-order QAT.

Recent architecture reports push the same conclusion from the systems side: quantization is no longer only a post-hoc compression step. FP8 and FP4 constraints are becoming part of model design, training, and post-training infrastructure.

## Conceptual Map

- **Weight-only PTQ:** W4A16-style methods quantize weights while leaving activations high precision. GPTQ and AWQ are strong here because the hardest activation outliers remain uncompressed.
- **Weight-activation PTQ:** W8A8 and W4A4 settings quantize both weights and activations. This is where SmoothQuant, Outlier Suppression, RPTQ, and OmniQuant address activation outliers and layer-wise reconstruction.
- **Data-free QAT:** LLM-QAT uses the full-precision model to generate synthetic training data, then quantizes weights, activations, and KV cache without needing the original pretraining corpus.
- **Efficient QAT:** EfficientQAT reduces QAT cost by splitting the problem: block-wise all-parameter reconstruction followed by end-to-end quantization-parameter tuning.
- **Forward-only QAT:** ZeroQAT replaces backpropagation with zeroth-order finite-difference estimates, keeping an end-to-end objective while avoiding activation-gradient storage.
- **Quantization-native training:** FP4/FP8-aware architecture and optimizer design treat low precision as a training constraint rather than a deployment afterthought.

The key trend is from local approximation toward global task alignment. Low-bit quantization fails when each layer looks locally reconstructed but the whole model's perplexity or downstream accuracy degrades.

## Mathematical Setup

A uniform affine quantizer maps full-precision tensor $X$ to integer levels:

$$
X_{\mathrm{int}} =
\mathrm{clip}\!\left(
\left\lfloor \frac{X}{s} \right\rceil + z,\,
q_{\min}, q_{\max}
\right),
\qquad
\widehat{X}=s(X_{\mathrm{int}}-z),
$$

where $s$ is the scale, $z$ the zero-point, and $\widehat{X}$ is the dequantized value used in the forward pass. Granularity matters: per-tensor scales are cheap but crude; per-channel and per-token scales better track LLM distributions but complicate kernels.

QAT keeps full-precision shadow weights $W$ but evaluates quantized weights $Q_\phi(W)$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{QAT}}(\theta,\phi)
= \mathbb{E}_{(x,y)}\left[
\ell(f_{Q_\phi(\theta)}(x),y)
\right].
$$

The straight-through estimator approximates the derivative through rounding:

$$
\frac{\partial Q(W)}{\partial W} \approx
\mathbf{1}\{q_{\min} < W/s + z < q_{\max}\}.
$$

ZeroQAT replaces this backpropagated update with a forward-only estimator:

$$
\widehat{\nabla} \mathcal{L}(W)
=
\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i=1}^{q}
\frac{\mathcal{L}(Q(W+\epsilon u_i))-\mathcal{L}(Q(W-\epsilon u_i))}
{2\epsilon}u_i,
\qquad u_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I).
$$

This makes the optimizer inference-like: two quantized forward passes per sampled direction rather than a full backward pass.

## Algorithms and Design Patterns

**Data-free QAT.**

1. Use the full-precision teacher to generate synthetic token sequences.
2. Quantize weights, activations, and optionally KV cache in the student.
3. Train the student to match teacher behavior under quantized forward passes.
4. Preserve outliers when clipping destroys early training dynamics.

LLM-QAT established this pattern and showed that W4A8KV4-style quantization can preserve zero-shot ability when the synthetic data and quantization choices are careful.

**Block-wise efficient QAT.**

1. Reconstruct one transformer block at a time against the full-precision teacher.
2. Optimize all block parameters, not just scales or clipping thresholds.
3. Freeze quantized weights.
4. Run end-to-end tuning on quantization parameters such as step sizes.

EfficientQAT's insight is that block-wise training handles intra-block approximation while a lightweight end-to-end phase handles cross-block error accumulation.

**Forward-only QAT.**

1. Keep full-precision shadow weights and quantizer parameters.
2. Run quantized forward passes under positive and negative perturbations.
3. Estimate gradients from loss differences.
4. Jointly update weights, clipping thresholds, zero-points, and smoothing transforms.

ZeroQAT extends SmoothQuant-style outlier migration by learning the scale and shift parameters end to end. This directly targets the two PTQ failure modes it diagnoses: cumulative layer-wise error and mismatch between local reconstruction loss and global task quality.

## Empirical Picture

The empirical ladder is bit-width dependent. At W6A6, several PTQ and efficient-QAT methods are close enough that kernel support and calibration cost may dominate the choice. At W4A4, the differences become large: local PTQ methods degrade sharply, while methods with weight adaptation and end-to-end alignment retain more quality.

LLM-QAT's evidence is strongest for data-free feasibility: generated sequences from the teacher can support QAT when original pretraining data is unavailable. EfficientQAT's evidence is about scale and cost: block-wise all-parameter training plus end-to-end scale tuning can quantize very large Llama-family models to 2-3 bits with small zero-shot drops. ZeroQAT's evidence is about low-bit weight-activation quantization and downstream fine-tuning: it reports consistent W4A4 perplexity and accuracy improvements over SmoothQuant, OS+, RPTQ, OmniQuant, and LLM-QAT, with especially large gains when the quantized model must also adapt to downstream tasks.

The benchmark pattern matters. Perplexity on WikiText2, PTB, and C4 is useful but incomplete. Zero-shot tasks test generalization; instruction tuning and downstream fine-tuning test whether quantization survives adaptation; long-context serving tests whether KV-cache quantization and kernels actually improve cost.

## Open Problems

- **Objective mismatch:** layer-wise reconstruction can improve while perplexity worsens.
- **Activation outliers:** preserving outliers helps quality, but serving kernels prefer regular ranges.
- **Calibration representativeness:** small calibration sets can miss rare but important activation regimes.
- **Kernel reality:** low-bit math is only useful when hardware and kernels exploit it end to end.
- **Quantized fine-tuning:** adapting a low-bit model is harder than only serving it.
- **KV-cache quantization:** long-context memory pressure makes cache precision as important as weight precision.
- **Interaction with MoE and agents:** routing, tool use, and long rollouts may stress quantized models differently from static benchmarks.
