# Literature Review: LLM Quantization

## Overview

Quantization replaces high-precision tensors (FP16/BF16) with low-bit integer or floating-point representations to shrink memory footprint, reduce bandwidth, and accelerate matmul-bound inference. For large language models the savings compound: a 70B FP16 model needs ~140 GB just for weights, while INT4 brings it under 40 GB and fits on a single A100-80GB.

The canonical uniform-quantization map is

$$
W_{\text{int}} = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{W}{s} \right\rceil + z,\; 0,\; 2^N - 1\right),\qquad \widehat{W} = (W_{\text{int}} - z)\cdot s,
$$

where $s$ is the scale, $z$ the zero-point, $N$ the bit-width, and $\widehat{W}$ the dequantized value used in forward pass. Symmetric quantization fixes $z=0$ and $s = \max(|W|)/(2^{N-1}-1)$. Granularity choices (per-tensor, per-channel, per-token, per-group) trade off accuracy against the kernel cost of carrying many scales.

Two regimes dominate the literature:

- **Post-Training Quantization (PTQ)** calibrates scales and rounding on a small dataset *after* training, never updating weights through gradients. GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant, and OmniQuant are PTQ workhorses. PTQ is cheap but degrades sharply below 4 bits.
- **Quantization-Aware Training (QAT)** simulates quantization during a fine-tuning pass via the *straight-through estimator* (STE): forward uses $\widehat{W}$, backward treats $\partial \widehat{W}/\partial W \approx 1$ inside the clip range. QAT recovers most of the low-bit accuracy gap but historically requires full training data and serious compute — both prohibitive for LLMs.

LLM-specific challenges that broke older recipes:

1. **Outliers.** A small fraction of activation channels carry magnitudes 100–1000× larger than the median. Naive clipping destroys information; SmoothQuant migrates the magnitude into weights, AWQ scales salient channels protectively.
2. **KV cache pressure.** At long context the KV cache dominates memory; quantizing K and V (e.g., INT4) is essential for throughput.
3. **No training data.** Pre-training corpora are often unreleased, multi-stage (SFT + RLHF), or legally encumbered, so QAT must work data-free or with synthetic substitutes.

Recent FP4-native training (e.g., NVIDIA Blackwell, DeepSeek-V4's FP4 QAT pipeline) blurs the line: quantization is no longer a post-hoc compression step but baked into the training loop itself.

## Key Concepts

- **Bit-width notation:** `WxAyKVz` means `x`-bit weights, `y`-bit activations, `z`-bit KV cache. W4A8KV4 is a common aggressive target.
- **STE (Straight-Through Estimator):** the trick that makes QAT differentiable through the rounding op.
- **Block-wise reconstruction:** rather than end-to-end fine-tuning, optimize one transformer block at a time against the FP teacher's output. Cheap and parallelizable; basis for GPTQ, OmniQuant, BRECQ.
- **Q-PEFT (Quantized PEFT):** freeze the quantized backbone, train a small set of continuous parameters (LoRA adapters, step sizes). QLoRA, QA-LoRA, PEQA.
- **Data-free distillation:** use the FP teacher to generate synthetic prompts/completions, distill the quantized student against them. Sidesteps the missing-data problem.
- **MinMax vs clipping quantizers:** MinMax preserves range (good when outliers matter); clipping reallocates precision to the bulk (good when outliers are rare).

## Papers

### LLM-QAT: Data-Free Quantization Aware Training for Large Language Models
{/* paper-file: 2024.findings-acl.26.pdf paper-hash: aad88e777fd4 */}
**Authors:** Zechun Liu, Barlas Oguz, Changsheng Zhao, Ernie Chang, Pierre Stock, Yashar Mehdad, Yangyang Shi, Raghuraman Krishnamoorthi, Vikas Chandra (Meta)
**Year:** 2024
**Source:** `inbox/papers/2024.findings-acl.26.pdf`

**Contribution:** First successful application of QAT to LLMs (LLaMA 7B/13B/30B) at sub-8-bit precision. Sidesteps the missing-pretraining-data problem with a data-free distillation pipeline that uses the FP teacher's own next-token generations as fine-tuning data, and shows that quantizing weights, activations, and the KV cache jointly down to W4A8KV4 is feasible while preserving zero-shot generalization.

**Key ideas:**
- **Data-free knowledge distillation:** sample from the pretrained model with a hybrid strategy — top-1 for the first 3–5 tokens (anchor confidence), then stochastic SoftMax sampling. 100k generated sequences are enough.
- **KV cache quantization is critical**, not optional: long-sequence throughput hinges on it.
- **Symmetric MinMax over clipping:** for LLMs with GLU-shaped distributions, clipping outliers during early QAT explodes perplexity past 10000 and never recovers; preserving outliers wins.
- **Per-token activation, per-channel weight** quantization granularity.
- Larger QAT'd models beat smaller FP16 models at matched ops (W4A8KV16 13B > W8A8KV16 7B).

**Connections:** First paper in this review. Establishes the QAT-for-LLMs baseline and the data-free pattern that EfficientQAT later inherits.

### EfficientQAT: Efficient Quantization-Aware Training for Large Language Models
{/* paper-file: 2407.11062v3.pdf paper-hash: e4036f5b554f */}
**Authors:** Mengzhao Chen, Wenqi Shao, Peng Xu, Jiahao Wang, Peng Gao, Kaipeng Zhang, Ping Luo (HKU, Shanghai AI Lab)
**Year:** 2024
**Source:** `inbox/papers/2407.11062v3.pdf`

**Contribution:** A two-stage QAT framework that closes most of the accuracy gap to full QAT at PTQ-level cost. Block-AP trains *all* parameters (weights, scales, zero-points) block-wise, then E2E-QP freezes weights and end-to-end trains only the step sizes to capture inter-block interactions. Quantizes Llama-2-70B to 2-bit on a single A100-80GB in 41 hours with under 3 points zero-shot accuracy drop.

**Key ideas:**
- **Block-AP** is the first method to train *all* original weights during block-wise reconstruction — prior work (OmniQuant, AutoRound, BRECQ, CBQ) restricted optimization to clipping thresholds, rounding, or LoRA adapters.
- **E2E-QP** keeps quantized weights fixed, trains only scales end-to-end. Memory cost stays small because gradients flow only through a handful of step-size parameters.
- The split exploits the fact that block-wise reconstruction handles intra-block precision, while end-to-end finishing handles cross-block error compounding.
- Outperforms both uniform PTQ (AutoRound, OmniQuant, DB-LLM) and Q-PEFT (QLoRA, QA-LoRA, PEQA) at 2- and 3-bit settings; competitive with vector-quantization methods (QUIP#, AQLM) without their kernel overhead.
- Works for instruction tuning too (+4.5 MMLU over PEQA on Alpaca).

**Connections:** Direct successor to LLM-QAT. Where LLM-QAT solved the *data* problem for QAT, EfficientQAT solves the *compute* problem — block-wise reconstruction lets you QAT a 70B model on one GPU. Together they make sub-4-bit LLMs practical.
